## 1. Purpose

State why the project exists and what it is intended to achieve.

Briefly describe:

-   the problem or opportunity
-   the proposed project
-   the intended organisational or operational outcome
-   why the work is being undertaken now

Reference the applicable Concept of Operations, Benefits Register and
stakeholder needs where available.

## 6. Approach and Major Work

Summarise how the project is expected to proceed.

Identify only the major work packages, phases or engineering activities
needed to understand the project.

Examples may include:

-   concept and requirements
-   data acquisition and assessment
-   model / system engineering
-   verification and validation
-   assurance and review
-   deployment
-   operational monitoring

Do not turn the charter into a detailed project schedule.

## 10. Governance and Decision Authority

State how the project is governed and who has authority for material
decisions.

Record, where applicable:

-   project sponsor / owner
-   project lead
-   technical authority
-   model / data owner
-   risk acceptance authority
-   release authority
-   escalation path
-   required review or approval points

Material technical decisions should be captured in `DEC-###` records
rather than buried in project-management notes.

## 11. Resources and Schedule

Summarise the resources and timing needed to establish that the project
is viable.

Record, where relevant:

-   expected project duration
-   major resource commitments
-   key specialist skills
-   infrastructure / compute requirements
-   material external costs
-   significant scheduling dependencies

Detailed resource plans and schedules may be maintained in
project-management tooling and need not be reproduced here.

## 12. Engineering Artefacts and Assurance

Identify which framework artefacts are expected to be used for this
project.

For a lightweight project this may simply be the Lean Practice Pack.
More complex, higher-risk or more heavily governed work may use selected
Full-Pack extensions.

Record any project-specific expectations for:

-   requirements traceability
-   decision records
-   architecture/design
-   data and model engineering
-   risk and controls
-   V&V
-   configuration/change control
-   release approval
-   operational monitoring

The charter authorises and frames the engineering work; it does not
replace the engineering evidence.

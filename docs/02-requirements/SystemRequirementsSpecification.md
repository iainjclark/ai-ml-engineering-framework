## Purpose

Define the functional, performance, non-functional and other system
requirements that the system shall satisfy.

This specification defines what the system must do and how well it must
perform, without unnecessarily prescribing how those requirements are
implemented.

## Scope

Describe the system, capability or release covered by this specification.

State important boundaries and anything explicitly outside scope.

## Stakeholder Needs and Intended Use

Summarise the stakeholder needs, benefits and intended uses from which the
requirements are derived.

Reference the corresponding `NEED-###`, `BEN-###` or other concept-stage IDs
where applicable.

## Assumptions and Constraints

Record assumptions and external constraints that materially affect the
requirements.

Examples may include:

- legal, regulatory or policy constraints
- technology or platform constraints
- cost or schedule constraints
- data availability
- operational environment
- human oversight requirements

## Requirements Quality

Requirements should be reviewed for:

- validity
- consistency
- completeness
- feasibility
- verifiability
- unnecessary implementation constraints

Requirements should be clear enough that objective evidence can later
demonstrate whether they have been satisfied.

## Traceability

Requirements shall have stable identifiers.

Trace requirements backward to their originating stakeholder need, benefit,
constraint or other source, and forward to verification activities and
evidence.

See `RequirementsTraceabilityMatrix.xlsx` for lifecycle traceability.
